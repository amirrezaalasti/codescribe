# Abstract checklist

## Pass 1 — Shape

- [ ] Context, gap, approach, results, and implication are all present (or deliberately omitted for a short venue)
- [ ] Each element gets roughly its allotted 1–2 sentences — no element eats the whole abstract
- [ ] The abstract reads as a standalone paragraph, understandable without the rest of the paper open

## Pass 2 — Substance

- [ ] Results are concrete: a number, a comparison, or a specific qualitative finding — not "promising results" or "significant improvement"
- [ ] The approach is named at the idea level (what makes it different), not just "a novel method"
- [ ] The gap is a specific missing capability, not "prior work has limitations"

## Pass 3 — Claim–evidence match

- [ ] Every claim in the abstract also appears, supported, in the body
- [ ] No result is stronger in the abstract than in the results/discussion section
- [ ] Scope words ("on X benchmarks", "for Y domain") match what was actually tested

## Pass 4 — Language hygiene

- [ ] No undefined acronyms
- [ ] No unsupported intensifiers ("novel", "unprecedented", "significantly" used loosely) without a number or citation backing them
- [ ] No citations unless the venue requires them
- [ ] Within the venue's stated word/character limit
